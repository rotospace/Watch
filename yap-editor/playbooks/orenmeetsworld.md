# Playbook: the yap playbook

Focus area: talking directly to camera — the core "why short-form talking-head
content works" reasoning that everything else in this folder builds on.

> Starter synthesis, not a transcript of any specific video. Replace with notes from
> whichever creator you actually study for this, the same way the original build for
> this system did — watch, take notes, rewrite this file.

## Core idea
Talking-head video is a conversation, not a presentation. The camera is a person, not
an audience — direct address ("you", eye contact with the lens) outperforms narration
almost every time.

## What to look for in a take
- Does it open like the middle of a thought the viewer just walked into, or like a
  speech with a preamble? Want the former.
- Is there one clear idea, or is the speaker hedging across two? One idea wins.
- Does the energy stay up through the last line, or trail off? Trailing off is a
  re-take flag, not something to fix in editing.

## Editing implications
- Cut anything that sounds like the speaker is reading a script out loud, even if it's
  well-delivered — flat delivery is a worse sin than an imperfect take with energy.
- Preserve micro-pauses that come right before the payoff; they're doing work, not
  wasting time.
