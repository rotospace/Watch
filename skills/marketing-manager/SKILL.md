# Marketing Manager

This skill was built by pointing `/watch` at a single source video
(Young Urban Project, *"Brand Manager vs Marketing Manager: Key
Differences"*) and merging its transcript with sampled frames into one
timeline before writing anything down. Claims below cite the transcript
timestamp they came from; anything not directly stated is marked as
inference. It's one creator's framing of two job titles, not an
industry standard -- treat it as a strong starting mental model, not
gospel.

## The core distinction [00:00:33-00:00:45]

- A brand manager thinks **long-term**; a marketing manager thinks
  **short-to-mid-term**.
- A brand manager's job is to **build meaning**; a marketing manager's
  job is to **drive results**.
- The marketing manager's operating question: *"How do we get more
  customers this month or this quarter?"* [00:01:11]

When you're using this skill, default to that question. If a request
is actually about what the brand should stand for, five-year
positioning, or brand personality, say so explicitly and flag that it's
brand-manager territory, not marketing-manager territory [00:01:03-00:01:11].

## What the role actually does [00:01:11-00:01:42]

- Campaign planning, lead generation, traffic, conversions, budgets, ROI.
- Runs ads day-to-day (brand managers usually don't).
- Decides **how** to push a message across channels -- not what the
  message is.
- Sits closer to execution than to strategy.

## How the role is measured [00:01:42]

Leads, sales, customer acquisition cost, return on ad spend (ROAS), and
campaign performance -- not brand awareness, recall, or long-term
equity (those are brand-manager metrics [00:01:42]).

When giving marketing-manager advice, prefer recommendations you can
tie to one of these numbers. If a suggestion can't be connected to a
lead, a sale, a CAC, or a ROAS movement, flag that it's a brand-side
move, not a marketing-manager one.

## Skills to lean on [00:02:03-00:02:16]

Execution speed, channel expertise, performance-marketing knowledge,
data analysis, budget management, an optimization mindset.

## When this path fits [00:02:49-00:03:01]

Marketing management fits someone who enjoys hands-on execution, likes
fast results and numbers, enjoys testing/optimizing/scaling, and wants
to specialize in a channel (ads, SEO, growth). Contrast: brand
management fits someone who prefers strategy over daily execution,
likes psychology and storytelling, and wants to shape long-term
direction [00:02:26-00:02:49].

## Career-path note (source's opinion, not a stated fact) [00:02:26-00:02:40]

The source claims marketing-manager roles are more common and easier to
enter early-career than brand-management roles, and that brand roles
pay more because they're fewer and more competitive. Treat this as one
creator's read on the market, not verified labor data -- a gap the
single-video sampling can't fill.

## Using this skill

When the user asks for marketing help:
1. Anchor on the operating question -- what moves a lead, a sale, a
   CAC, or a ROAS number this month/quarter.
2. Give channel- and execution-level recommendations (the "how"), not
   brand-positioning advice (the "what"), unless they've explicitly
   asked to cross into brand territory.
3. If they're asking a career question ("should I go into marketing or
   brand management?"), walk them through the fit criteria above rather
   than picking for them.
